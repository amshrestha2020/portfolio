Neural Network Playground

Einstein is said to have said: if you cannot explain it simply, you do not understand it well enough. So here is a neural network in plain words, and a tiny one you can train yourself.

A neural network in plain words

A neural network is a team of very simple decision-makers arranged in layers. Each one looks at some numbers, treats some of them as more important than others (these importance scores are called weights), adds them up, and decides how loudly to speak up (that rule is the activation function).

The first layer sees the raw input, like the pixels of a photo. Later layers combine those signals into bigger ideas: edges, then shapes, then "this is a cat". At first the team guesses badly. Each time, we tell the team how wrong it was, and every member nudges its weights a tiny bit to be less wrong next time. After thousands of nudges, the team gets good. That is learning: practice with feedback, not memorizing rules.

1. InputThe numbers go in (here: a point's x and y).
2. Hidden layerLittle deciders weigh the inputs and respond.
3. OutputThe team gives an answer: orange or blue.
4. FeedbackWe measure the error and nudge the weights. Repeat.
