  1. 1950
    Golden years

    Turing asks if machines can think

    Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" and proposes a text conversation test in which a judge cannot tell machine from human. It becomes the Turing Test.

  2. 1956
    Golden years

    Dartmouth workshop names the field

    A summer workshop at Dartmouth College brings researchers together, and John McCarthy introduces the term "artificial intelligence".

  3. 1956
    Golden years

    Logic Theorist and a checkers learner

    Newell and Simon build programs that prove logic theorems, and Arthur Samuel's checkers player learns well enough to beat its own developer.

  4. 1957
    Golden years

    The Mark I Perceptron

    Frank Rosenblatt demonstrates a single-layer neural network at Cornell that sorts input into two categories.

  5. 1958
    Golden years

    LISP

    McCarthy's LISP becomes the first programming language of AI research.

  6. 1966
    Golden years

    ELIZA, the first chatbot

    Joseph Weizenbaum's ELIZA at MIT holds scripted conversations and shows how readily people read understanding into text.

  7. 1966
    Golden years

    ALPAC report on translation

    A U.S. review finds machine translation more expensive, less accurate and slower than human translation, an early sign that promises were outrunning results.

  8. 1969
    Golden years

    Perceptrons exposes a limit

    Minsky and Papert show that basic perceptrons cannot handle problems like XOR, and neural network research loses support for years.

  9. 1973
    First AI winter

    The Lighthill report

    Sir James Lighthill reviews British AI research and finds it short of its goals. A BBC debate the same year contests his conclusions, but the funding consequences stand.

  10. 1973–74
    First AI winter

    DARPA pulls back

    U.S. defence funding for open-ended academic AI shrinks and shifts toward applied projects with measurable goals.

  11. 1982
    Expert systems boom

    Hopfield networks

    John Hopfield's network stores and recalls patterns using ideas borrowed from physics, bringing neural networks back into view.

  12. 1986
    Expert systems boom

    Backpropagation for hidden layers

    Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams popularize backpropagation, the training method behind later deep learning.

  13. 1987
    Second AI winter

    The LISP machine market collapses

    The billion-dollar AI hardware and software industry begins to fall apart as expert systems prove costly and brittle.

  14. 1989
    Second AI winter

    Backpropagation reads zip codes

    Yann LeCun applies backpropagation to recognizing handwritten zip codes, an early practical use of the convolutional approach.

  15. 1997
    Machine learning rebuilds

    Deep Blue beats Kasparov

    On May 11, IBM's Deep Blue wins a six-game match against world chess champion Garry Kasparov, 3.5 to 2.5, relying mainly on programmed chess knowledge and brute-force search.

  16. 2000
    Machine learning rebuilds

    Word embeddings

    Yoshua Bengio's neural probabilistic language model represents word meaning as dense vectors, a step toward modern language AI.

  17. 2006
    Machine learning rebuilds

    CUDA opens GPUs to general computing

    Nvidia ships the CUDA platform in November, and researchers find GPUs well suited to the matrix math inside neural networks.

  18. 2011
    Machine learning rebuilds

    Watson wins Jeopardy!

    IBM's Watson beats champions Ken Jennings and Brad Rutter and can parse natural-language questions.

  19. 2012
    Deep learning & cloud

    AlexNet wins ImageNet

    A deep convolutional network trained on two Nvidia GPUs cuts top-5 error to 15.3%, against 26.2% for the runner-up. Deep learning becomes the default approach in vision.

  20. 2016
    Deep learning & cloud

    AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol

    DeepMind's AlphaGo beats the world Go champion by combining deep learning with reinforcement learning, trained in the cloud on Google's TPU chips.

  21. 2017
    Deep learning & cloud

    The Transformer

    The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the architecture behind today's large language models.

  22. 2020
    Deep learning & cloud

    AlphaFold 2 and GPT-3

    AlphaFold 2 predicts protein structures accurately, and OpenAI's GPT-3 shows how far scale alone can carry a language model.

  23. 2022
    Deep learning & cloud

    ChatGPT reaches everyone

    In November, OpenAI opens a free preview of ChatGPT, built on GPT-3.5, and generative AI becomes a mainstream tool within weeks.

  24. 2024
    Deep learning & cloud

    Nobel Prizes recognize AI

    Hinton and Hopfield share the Physics prize for foundational work on artificial neural networks, and Hassabis and Jumper share half of the Chemistry prize for AlphaFold 2, with David Baker honored for protein design.

What caused the AI winters

First AI winter (1974 – 1980)

Causes: The 1973 Lighthill report judged AI to have failed its big promises, DARPA cut general academic AI funding in 1973 and 1974, and machine translation and perceptron limits had already dented confidence.

Cost: UK AI research was largely dismantled outside a few universities such as Edinburgh, Essex and Sussex, and DARPA redirected money to projects with specific, measurable goals. The pattern: pessimism among researchers, then in the press, then a funding cut.

Second AI winter (1987 – 1993)

Causes: The LISP machine market collapsed in 1987, and hand-written rule bases proved brittle, with maintenance costs growing beyond the value they returned.

Cost: Companies abandoned expert systems and funding and enthusiasm fell again, though some work continued underneath, including neural networks applied to real tasks. Dating varies by source.

Why this matters to you

The two winters show what happens when hype outruns measurable results, and the revival shows that progress returns once data, hardware and methods catch up. As an engineer with 5+ years of production backend and cloud experience in financial services and graduate-level AI training, I focus on building AI-enabled systems that are reliable, secure, explainable and tied to measurable goals.

1. Machine learning vs. deep learning

Machine learning learns patterns from data, often with features that people choose. Deep learning uses many-layered neural networks that learn those features themselves, which is why it excels on images, speech and language but needs more data and computing power.

🔄

2. Adaptability and change leadership

Adopting AI is a people change as much as a technical one. I learned to stay flexible as tools evolve, bring colleagues along with clear communication, and lead change by showing small, measurable wins first.

Flight Simulator

Problem: Flying is hard to picture from a textbook. Lift, drag, stalls and landings only make sense when you feel them.
Approach: I built a simple 3D flight simulator that runs in the browser. You take off or land a small plane with real lift, drag, stall and ground effect, with a coach that explains what is happening.
Outcome: A playable simulator with keyboard and touch controls, deployed on GitHub Pages. From inside it you can open the Helicopter Rotor Lab below.

Helicopter Rotor Lab

Problem: It is hard to see how a helicopter blade pushes air, and what you give up when you change the design.
Approach: Animated airflow through the rotor and over one blade. Change blade count, length, width, speed, pitch and height, then read the pros and cons and the before-and-after numbers.
Outcome: A learning tool using a simplified blade-element and momentum model (baseline close to a light four-seat helicopter).
